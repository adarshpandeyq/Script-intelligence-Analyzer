THE THIRD NIGHT

A Short Horror Screenplay
Written for NLP / Script Analysis Testing


FADE IN:

INT. THERAPY ROOM - NIGHT

A single desk lamp burns. Rain hammers the window. DR. EVELYN HOLLOWAY (50s, calm, careful) sits opposite MARA BLACKWOOD (29, exhausted, eyes that have not slept).

                    MARA
          It is always the same dream. The hallway
          is very long, and the door at the end of
          it is always closed.

                    DR. HOLLOWAY
          The same door every time?

                    MARA
          The same door. Three long scratches in
          the paint, low down, like something
          tried to claw its way out.

Mara's hands shake. She hides them under the table.

                    MARA (CONT'D)
          There is music, too. A music box. Cheap,
          tinny, playing the same eight notes
          forever. I wake up before I reach the
          door. Every time.
              (beat)
          I wake up before I reach the door, and
          that is the only reason I am still
          alive.

                    DR. HOLLOWAY
          Alive?

                    MARA
          In the dream, I am not alone in the
          hallway. Something is behind me. It
          breathes like a person, but it breathes
          wrong.

Holloway writes something down. The pen hesitates.

                    DR. HOLLOWAY
          Your father's house has been empty for
          eleven years, Mara.

                    MARA
          Twelve years and nine months.

                    DR. HOLLOWAY
          And still you are going back.

                    MARA
          I have to. The lawyer said the house
          transfers at midnight on the third
          night. If I am not there, it goes to
          the state.
              (whispering)
          I am not letting it go to the state.

CUT TO:

EXT. COUNTRY ROAD - DUSK

An old sedan crawls along a cracked road. Pine trees crowd the shoulder. MARA drives, knuckles white.

The RADIO cuts through static.

                    RADIO ANNOUNCER (V.O.)
          ...and the storm warning remains in
          effect for Cedar Hollow County through
          Wednesday. Residents are urged to stay
          indoors after dark. Sheriff's office
          also reminds everyone that the old
          Blackwood property remains condemned -

Mara switches it off. Silence. Then, very quietly, from the back seat:

                    MARA
              (to herself)
          Three nights. That is all. Three
          nights and I never come back.

CUT TO:

EXT. BLACKWOOD HOUSE - NIGHT

The house sits in a clearing like a broken tooth. Porch sagging. Windows boarded from the inside.

Mara steps out of the car. Wind tears at her coat. She aims her flashlight at the front door.

A CELLAR DOOR, half buried in dirt at the side of the house, hangs open a hand's width. A heavy padlock dangles, unbroken, from the hasp.

Mara freezes. She stares at the gap.

                    MARA
              (barely a breath)
          No.

She crosses the yard. Mud sucks at her boots. She kneels at the cellar door and shines the light inside.

Three long scratches run down the inside of the door, low down, in the old paint.

Mara recoils, falls backward into the mud.

                    MARA (CONT'D)
          That is a dream. That is only a dream.

She slams the cellar door shut, drags a rusted plow blade across it, and wedges it tight.

CUT TO:

INT. BLACKWOOD HOUSE - HALLWAY - NIGHT

The front door opens with a long groan. Dust. Cold air that does not belong to the season.

Mara steps in. Her flashlight sweeps over sheeted furniture, a staircase, and -

At the far end of the hallway, a DOOR. Painted shut. Low, at the bottom: three long scratches.

                    MARA
          Okay. Okay. It is a house. It is just
          a house.

She forces herself forward. The floorboards answer her, one at a time.

A small table against the wall holds a MUSIC BOX, cedar and brass, the size of a fist.

Mara picks it up.

                    MARA (CONT'D)
              (small)
          I remember you.

She winds it. Eight thin notes.

Mara's breath stops. She sets the box down very gently, as if it were sleeping.

CUT TO:

INT. BLACKWOOD HOUSE - KITCHEN - NIGHT

Mara tears open a drawer. Old bills, a flashlight with dead batteries, and -

A LETTER, yellow, folded four times.

                    MARA
              (reading)
          "Mara. If you are reading this, I am
          gone, and I did not get to say the
          thing I should have said."

Mara sinks into a chair.

                    MARA (CONT'D)
              (reading)
          "There is a door in the cellar. Do not
          open it. Not on the first night, not on
          the second. Whatever you hear down
          there, it sounds like family. It is
          not."

Her hand covers her mouth.

                    MARA (CONT'D)
              (reading)
          "On the third night, it stops
          pretending. Lock the house. Lock
          yourself in the front room. Do not
          answer if it says your name..."

The letter continues. Mara reads on, faster now.

                    MARA (CONT'D)
              (reading)
          "...do not answer if it says your name,
          because it will sound exactly like
          Elias."

The paper trembles.

                    MARA
          Elias is dead.

Outside, thunder. The kitchen light - there is no power - flickers anyway, once, and dies.

                    MARA (CONT'D)
              (to the dark)
          Elias is dead.

CUT TO:

INT. BLACKWOOD HOUSE - BEDROOM - NIGHT

Mara lies fully dressed on a bare mattress, flashlight clutched like a weapon.

SLEEP WILL NOT COME. Then it does.

IN THE DREAM: the hallway. The door. The breathing behind her.

MARA (V.O.)
          Do not turn around. Do not turn around.
          Do not -

A WHISPER, close, warm, a child's voice:

                    THE VOICE
              (whispering)
          Mara. You left me down here.

Mara wakes, gasping.

Downstairs, in the dark, the MUSIC BOX begins to play. Eight thin notes. Then again. Then again.

CUT TO:

INT. BLACKWOOD HOUSE - HALLWAY - NIGHT

Mara creeps down the stairs, one bare foot at a time. The music has stopped.

Her phone light finds the music box on the table. Lid open. Still.

                    MARA
              (shaking)
          Second night. Everyone. Just the
          second night.

Her phone BUZZES. No signal bars. A message, timestamped nine years ago, from a number saved as ELIAS.

                    MARA (CONT'D)
              (reading the screen)
          "Mar - the box is playing again. It
          plays when he is hungry. Tell Dad. Tell
          Dad please. It is dark down here."

Mara's legs give out.

                    MARA (CONT'D)
              (a broken sound)
          No. No, you died. You drowned. They
          had a funeral. I carried the -

The music box SNAPS shut by itself.

From behind the painted door at the end of the hall, three slow scratches drag down the wood.

CUT TO:

INT. CELLAR STAIRS - NIGHT

Mara descends. The flashlight beam shakes. Earth walls. Each step is a decision she regrets.

At the bottom: a chair, a chain, a bucket. A child's drawings taped to the wall, dozens of them, all the same - a house, a door, and a stick figure outside the house looking in.

Mara touches one.

                    MARA
              (a whisper)
          You were down here the whole time.

The music box is here, on the floor, wound, waiting.

Behind her, at the top of the stairs, the cellar door SWINGS SHUT.

Darkness. Absolute.

CUT TO:

INT. CELLAR - NIGHT

The flashlight flickers back to life.

Something stands in the corner. Roughly the shape of a man. The wrong breathing.

                    THE FIGURE
              (warm, familiar)
          You came back.

                    MARA
          Who are you?

                    THE FIGURE
          You know who I am. You have known since
          the hallway.

It steps into the light. The face is her father's. The voice is not.

                    THE FIGURE (CONT'D)
          He locked me down here for eleven
          years. He told you I drowned. You
          believed him because you were little
          and because believing him was easier
          than listening.

Mara backs into the wall. Her hand finds the drawings.

                    MARA
          Elias?

                    THE FIGURE
              (gently)
          Third night, Mara. You should have
          locked the front room.

The music box PLAYS. Eight notes. Louder. The chain on the floor begins to drag toward her.

                    THE FIGURE (CONT'D)
          Now you stay. Now someone else leaves.

Mara screams. The flashlight dies.

CUT TO:

INT. THERAPY ROOM - DAY

Sunlight. Birdsong. The lamp is off.

Mara sits exactly where she sat before, drenched in mud, hair matted, hands bleeding.

DR. HOLLOWAY stares at her.

                    DR. HOLLOWAY
          Mara... you never left this chair.

Mara looks down. Her palms are torn, packed with dirt from a cellar floor eleven miles away.

                    MARA
              (hoarse)
          It is the third night.

                    DR. HOLLOWAY
          You have been speaking for four hours.
          You told me all of it. Every word.

                    MARA
          Then it heard me say all of it.

Holloway reaches for the phone. Mara catches her wrist - too hard.

                    MARA
          Do not call anyone. It only needs
          someone to answer.

                    DR. HOLLOWAY
          Answer what?

                    MARA
          Its name.

A long silence. Somewhere beyond the office door, in the hallway outside, eight thin notes begin to play.

Holloway's eyes go wide.

                    DR. HOLLOWAY
          There is no music box in this building.

Three slow scratches drag down the outside of the office door.

Mara closes her eyes.

                    MARA
              (a whisper)
          I know.

FADE OUT.

THE END
